Driving the treads from rocky-pi

Started 2026-10-06 · Revision 1 (2026-10-06) — first draft for Pat's review · status: awaiting approval · follows on from Tread drive over Bluetooth (its decision D7)

In one paragraph

Rocky's brain asks the body service on rocky-pi to drive: a left speed, a right speed and a length of time. The body service hands that to a small drive driver. Today's driver passes it to the mood bridge, which already holds the Bluetooth link to each badge, and the badge turns it into infrared for the LEGO receiver. Which badge drives is one setting. A different kind of motor hardware later means a new driver, not a new way of asking.

How a drive command travels

Decisions

DecisionWhy
D1The mood bridge carries drive commands, for any badge it knows.It already owns the one Bluetooth link each badge allows. The face badge can then show moods and drive on the same link.
D2The body service gets a general POST /drive, with a swappable driver behind it.Pat, 6 October: "we're gonna need that kind of control when we get the driver boards or whatever other solution we come up with". The brain asks the same way whatever moves the treads.
D3Which badge drives is a setting in the body service (the bridge's name for that badge).Pat chose "either, chosen by a setting".
D4The bridge does the fast resending; the body service sends one request per move.The badge needs a fresh command about every 0.2 s. The bridge sits next to the Bluetooth link, so Wi-Fi hiccups between the Mac and the Pi cannot starve it.
D5Every move has a length, capped at 5 seconds (a setting). A longer drive is several moves.A brain that goes quiet cannot leave the base running.
D6The body service's existing /stop also stops the treads, and a new move replaces the one running.One stop for the whole body; a stop always wins.
D7Driving does not depend on the NXT brick. If the brick is unreachable the treads still work, and the reverse.They are separate hardware; one failing should not take the other down.
D8The brain gets a drive call on its body client and nothing more for now. Teaching Rocky when to drive is a later design.Keeps this piece small and testable.
D9No firmware change.The badges already do everything this needs.

Approaches considered

ApproachVerdict
Bridge carries drive, body service fronts it (chosen)Works for either badge; one more local hop; two projects change.
Body service opens its own Bluetooth link to the badgeFine for the spare, impossible for the face badge while the bridge holds its link. Fails "either".
Brain calls the bridge directly, no body serviceFewer parts, but the brain would then be tied to badges, which is what D2 avoids.

Part 1: the mood bridge (pi-arduino-face-emotions)

EndpointBodyDoes
POST /drivetarget, left, right (−7 to 7, one decimal), ms (100 to 10000)Starts a background task that sends the command every 0.2 s with a 0.6 s lifetime until ms is up, then sends a stop. Answers at once. Replaces any move already running for that badge. Both speeds 0 means stop.
DELETE /drive?target=Cancels the running move and sends a stop.
GET /drive?target=Reads the badge's 8-byte drive status: driving, infrared ready, low memory, rejected, speeds, time left, battery, free memory.
  • Same key check, same per-badge lock and same error answer (502 when the badge is out of reach) as the mood endpoints.
  • The 11-byte command and 8-byte status are built and read by small pure functions, copied in meaning from pf_ble_drive.py and tested without Bluetooth.
  • The three drive addresses (UUIDs) join the mood ones in config.py, each overridable by an environment variable.
  • A badge whose firmware has no drive service answers 502 with a plain message; moods on it are unaffected.
  • The move task ends quietly if the badge drops out; the badge stops by itself within 0.6 s.

Part 2: the body service (rocky-robot)

# ~/.config/rocky-body/settings.toml on rocky-pi
[drive]
kind = "badge"                      # "badge", "fake", or leave the section out for no treads
bridge_url = "http://127.0.0.1:8026"
target = "tread"                    # the bridge's name for the badge that drives
max_ms = 5000
max_speed = 7.0

The bridge's key comes from the environment (ROCKY_DRIVE_BRIDGE_KEY), never from the settings file.

PieceWhat it does
body/service/drive.pyThe driver shape (drive(left, right, ms), stop(), state()), a BadgeDrive that calls the bridge, and a FakeDrive for tests and for running without hardware.
settings.pyReads and checks the [drive] section.
app.pyPOST /drive; /stop stops treads as well as joints; /state gains a drive part. No [drive] section means /drive answers 404 "this body has no treads".
rocky/body/client.pyawait body.drive(left, right, ms).

Anyone calls /stopThe move is cancelled and a stop is sentabout 0.1 s

Security

  • Both endpoints keep their existing key checks. The body service's port is open to the home network, as it is today; the bridge stays reachable only from rocky-pi itself for this path.
  • This adds a way to make a physical thing move from the network, so the limits (speed, length) are enforced in the body service, not trusted from the caller.
  • known gap The badge's 8-character secret is fixed in the firmware source and sent unencrypted over Bluetooth. It now guards motion. Fixing it (a per-badge secret and an encrypted link) is a separate piece of work and is not part of this design.

Open points

  • Each badge's Bluetooth address as rocky-pi sees it. Found by a scan on the Pi at stage 3; it is not the same as the USB hardware address.
  • Battery percent reads 0 in the badge's drive status. Reported as-is for now.
  • The face badge as the driver needs its top edge to see the receiver. A mounting question, not a software one.
  • Named moves ("forward a foot", "quarter turn left"), like the arm's named poses, would suit Rocky's brain. Left for the design that teaches Rocky when to drive.
